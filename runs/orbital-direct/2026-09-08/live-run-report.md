# Pilot A — LIVE research run

_Orbital Direct · 2026-09-08 · `npm run pilot-a-live`_

The question this run answers: **can Signal independently rediscover current,
evidence-backed commercial triggers without the hand-built fixture?**

Broadly yes, with one important qualification — the live run was *better* than
the fixture in three places and *worse* in one, and the worse case was caused
by source classification rather than by research quality.

---

## How it was run

Queries were generated by `buildQueries` from the client's own demand-trigger
model and executed **verbatim**. The engine chose what to search; nothing was
hand-picked. Extraction was done from the fresh results.

No search API is reachable from the engine process: the egress gateway returns
403 to `CONNECT` for `api.search.brave.com`, `html.duckduckgo.com`,
`lite.duckduckgo.com` and `bing.com`. The live path therefore ran over an
**agent bridge** — the agent executed the engine's queries and returned the
results. `HttpSearchClient` is the real implementation for any environment with
search access, and is tested against stubbed provider responses.

The bridge's integrity rule: **an uncaptured query raises rather than returning
an empty list.** A silent empty result would manufacture a false "researched
and found nothing", which is the one negative result that must always be real.

### Chains

**Maeving — 70 (raw 87).** Facts: £3m UKEF-backed HSBC facility for the US,
Germany and France with 13 new jobs (gov.uk, tier 2, 2026-08-15); exporting

(thegrowthhub.me, tier 4). One inference: consignment volume on established
lanes is rising steeply with funded capacity behind it. Hypothesis: freight
arrangements sized for 2023 volumes are carrying several times that, on a
regulated cargo type. Test: ask whether the current arrangement was priced
before the fivefold increase.

### False positives found in the fixture

**Maeving's "domestic-first shipper" inference was wrong.** The fixture reasoned
that a company "that has until now shipped mainly domestically" would hit
freight problems at scale. Live research found Maeving has exported since 2023,
ships roughly half its output abroad, and the US is already its largest
international market. The signal is real but the framing was false, and the
sales angle built on it would have been wrong in front of the prospect. The
live run carries the correction as a visible contradiction rather than quietly
rewriting it, and drops signal strength from 19 to 16.

**No false positives were produced by the live run** — but see Slack & Parr,
which is the near miss the contradiction pass caught.

### Missed opportunities in the fixture

- **Baltex's Boeing approval and USA market entry.** The fixture had only the
  finance story. A new market entry is a materially stronger trigger than
  growth on an existing lane, and the live run reclassified the trigger from
  `export_finance` to `new_market_entry` on that basis.
- **Maeving's £8m raise** (September 2025), missed entirely by the fixture.
- **deVOL's new markets in Thailand, China and Denmark**, 31% of sales
  exported, overseas revenue up 2,300% over six years. Far stronger than the
  fixture's award-only framing.
- **deVOL's £1.95m purchase** of a 40,000 sq ft Shepshed factory (undated).

## New failure modes

**1. Identity collision on name-only queries — 3 of 7 companies.**
The engine appended a location qualifier only to its first query. The trigger
queries used bare company names and collided badly:

- "Bramble Group export growth…" → **Brambles Ltd**, the Australian CHEP pallet
  company.
- "NMS International Group export growth…" → **NMS Industries**, a Chinese
  mining equipment maker, and NMS Consulting.
- "Slack & Parr export growth…" → **academic papers on organisational slack**.

A less careful extractor would have attributed Brambles' 60-country operation
to a Market Harborough food business. **Fixed**: every query now carries a
location or industry qualifier by default. Verified live — re-running the
Bramble query with "Market Harborough" appended returned only the correct
company, and surfaced a genuine extra fact (Whitakers Chocolates acquired
January 2025).

**2. A company's own site on a different TLD is not recognised as first-party.**
deVOL's `devolkitchens.co.uk` scored tier 4 against a target declaring
`devolkitchens.com`. Combined with `kbbfocus.com` being an unknown trade title
(also tier 4), the row hit the aggregator-only cap of 50 and was dropped —
**despite the live research being substantially richer than the fixture's**.
Added `aliasDomains` to company identity so owned domains can be declared. The
live run was deliberately **not** re-scored with the alias applied; doing so
would lift deVOL to a reportable score, and that decision should be made
openly rather than folded into a run that is also being compared to a fixture.

**3. Source classification is now the binding constraint on score, not
research quality.** deVOL is the proof: better facts, worse score. The
conservative unknown-host default is right in principle — over-trusting an
unknown source is worse than under-scoring a good one — but the registry is
too small, and every unknown host is silently costing real signals. The fix is
to grow the registry from evidence, not to relax the default.

**4. Contraction signals are real, current, and the opposite of an
opportunity.** Slack & Parr surfaced a well-sourced August 2026 event: a
consultation on up to 40 redundancies driven by slowing Chinese and Far East
investment, tariffs in new export markets, and rising domestic costs. It is
current, ICP-fitting and commercially significant — and it means freight demand
is falling. The engine rejected it via a fatal contradiction, which works, but
the signal taxonomy has no concept of **polarity**. A signal type should be
able to declare that it reduces demand, rather than relying on a researcher to
mark a contradiction.

## What this run establishes

The loop rediscovers real triggers from generated queries, and on this evidence
it reasons better than the hand-built fixture: it corrected a wrong inference,
found three signals the fixture missed, and declined to invent a signal for NMS
where the fixture had scored one at 56.

The binding constraints are now, in order: **source verification** (everything
caps at 70 without page fetching), **source classification coverage**, and
**query disambiguation** (fixed). None of them is a reasoning problem.
