# Human grading pack — five briefs for an Orbital salesperson

_Prepared 2026-09-10 from the run of 2026-09-09. Engine unchanged._
_Live version (fillable): the published artifact. This file is the record._

## Two fairness problems found while preparing this, and how they were handled

**1. There is no call sheet of five.** In the honest run — the one where no page
was ever retrieved — the engine recommends contacting **nobody**. Baltex and
Maeving are "verify before contact"; deVOL, Bramble and NMS are "monitor".
Presenting five as leads would have misrepresented the output. All five are
presented with their real verdicts, held to the foot of each brief so the
salesperson forms a view first. The briefs are ordered alphabetically, not by
rank, for the same reason.

**2. deVOL's brief contains an unsourced claim.** Signal's `whatChanged`, sales
angle, "why now" and suggested question all assert that deVOL has "established
new overseas markets in **Thailand, China and Denmark**". No supporting fact
says so. The three cited facts are: the King's Award with 2,300% overseas growth
and 31% exported; projects in 26 countries; a product launched in Taipei.

The chain validator checks the *structure* of fact → inference → hypothesis. It
does not check that the free-text fields a salesperson actually reads stay
within the facts. So an unevidenced assertion reaches the output unflagged —
the precise failure the project exists to prevent.

**Handled by annotation, not alteration.** The brief shows what Signal produced
and flags the three markets as unsupported. Nothing was edited to look better,
no evidence was created, no label changed. Recorded here as a defect for after
the grading pass.

## What is being tested

Whether these briefs would change what a salesperson does on Monday morning.
Not whether the engine is well built.

## Brief A · Baltex (W. Ball & Son) — Ilkeston, technical textiles

**Why there may be an opportunity.** Boeing approval cleared and the USA named
as a new target market. A seven-figure HSBC package announced 20 July 2026
funding EU trade, with a stated target of ~20% export growth over twelve
months. Exports are already 60% of the business.

**Evidence** (all search-result summaries; no page opened)
- Targeting the USA after Boeing approval; 20% export growth expected; 60%
  export; agents in Hong Kong, Italy, Finland and the USA —
  `knittingindustry.com/uks-baltex-accelerates-international-strategy/`
- Seven-figure HSBC UK package; Polish subsidiary since 2008 —
  `innovationintextiles.com/baltex-accelerates-growth-with-hsbc-uk-support/`
- Funding spent on three appointments and three AI-integrated machines, split
  Ilkeston/Poland — `themanufacturer.com/articles/baltex-secures-seven-figure-funding-to-drive-european-expansion/`

**Likely angle.** First shipments into an aerospace supply chain — documentation
and traceability — before one goes.

**Uncertainty.** Every article traces to one HSBC release. **The same article
says Baltex already has agents in the USA**, so "a lane they have not run
before" may be wrong. Aerospace technical textiles are low-volume/high-value;
the freight spend may be small.

**Suggested reason for contact.** Ask whether US shipments have started and who
handled the first ones.

**Signal's verdict:** verify before contact. Ranked 1st of 5 on value, held back
on evidence.

## Brief B · Bramble Group (Bramble Foods) — Market Harborough, fine food

**Why there may be an opportunity.** Opened Lancaster House, a purpose-built
67,000 sq ft distribution centre, on 31 July 2026 — its main UK hub and third
base in the town, ~£500k invested. Around 300 staff, up to 50 more planned.
Acquired Whitakers Chocolates (Jan 2025) and The Bay Tree (Jan 2024).

**Evidence** (all search-result summaries; no page opened)
- 67,000 sq ft national DC at Airfield Business Park, becomes main UK hub —
  `grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/07/31/bramble-foods-opens-new-distribution-hub-and-eyes-expansion/`
- Third base in the town, ~£500k, kept growth in Market Harborough —
  `harboroughmail.co.uk/business/...-8832307`
- ~300 staff, up to 50 new roles — `themanufacturer.com/articles/bramble-foods-expansion-to-create-50-new-jobs-in-leicestershire/`
- July 2026 Lancaster House; Jan 2025 Whitakers; Jan 2024 Bay Tree — `bramblefoods.com`

**Likely angle.** Not storage — they have just built their own. Q4 overflow and
outbound haulage across three brands, as a September conversation.

**Uncertainty.** **The change cuts both ways**: a company opening its own main
hub needs *less* third-party storage. Nothing says they were short of overflow
last November, or that haulage is contracted out. The acquisitions are 20 months
old.

**Suggested reason for contact.** Ask what they did for overflow last November,
and whether Lancaster House changes that answer.

**Signal's verdict:** monitor, do not call. Best-evidenced entry (4 sources, firm
date), weakest commercial case. Included deliberately to test the rejection.

## Brief C · deVOL Kitchens — Cotes Mill, Loughborough

**Why there may be an opportunity.** Exports 31% of sales; overseas revenue up
2,300% over six years from virtually nothing; projects in 26 countries; every
kitchen still made in Leicestershire; a product launched in Taipei. Roughly two
miles from Orbital.

**Evidence** (all search-result summaries; no page opened)
- 2,300% overseas growth, 31% exported, King's Award for International Trade —
  `devolkitchens.com/blog/we-won-a-kings-award` — dated 6 May 2026, **but that
  dates the award, not the growth**
- Projects in 26 countries, all made in Leicestershire —
  `devolkitchens.co.uk/blog/overseas-kitchen-projects-faqs` — no date
- All-In-One Island launched in Taipei —
  `devolkitchens.co.uk/blog/the-all-in-one-island-by-devol-launches-in-taipei` — no date

**Likely angle.** Bulky, fragile, high-value cabinetry where crating decides
whether it arrives saleable; Asian and Nordic destinations crate differently to
New York; Orbital is close enough to inspect a load before it ships.

**Uncertainty.** **Nothing here is dated** — there is no "why now". **Signal's
own summary names Thailand, China and Denmark as new markets; no cited source
supports that** (see the fairness note above). An exporter at 31% has forwarders
already.

**Suggested reason for contact.** Ask who handles the consignments into Asia and
whether that is the same arrangement as the US.

**Signal's verdict:** monitor, do not call. Ranked 3rd of 5, downgraded because
nothing dates the change.

## Brief D · Maeving — Sibree Road, Coventry, electric motorcycles

**Why there may be an opportunity.** £3m UKEF-backed trade finance facility
announced 15 August 2026 to build capacity for the US, Germany and France,
adding 13 jobs. US sales up fivefold year on year. Exporting since 2023, ships
around half its bikes. Raised a further £11m in March 2026.

**Evidence** (all search-result summaries; no page opened)
- £3m HSBC/UKEF facility, US/Germany/France, 13 jobs at Coventry —
  `gov.uk/government/news/maeving-in-the-right-direction-e-motorbike-maker-gears-up-exports-with-ukef-backing`
- Exporting to California, Germany, France since 2023; ~half its bikes; US sales
  up fivefold vs 2024 despite tariffs — `motorcyclenews.com/news/2026/august/maeving-get-3m-government-backed-cash-injection/`
- £11m raised March 2026, incl. West Midlands Co-Investment Fund —
  `britishdealernews.co.uk/news/uk/maeving-wins-11m-funding-boost/`

**Likely angle.** Volume on a named lane has multiplied, and every bike carries
a lithium battery — Class 9 dangerous goods packing and paperwork. *The battery
point is inferred from the product; no source states it.*

**Uncertainty.** Exporting since 2023 and shipping half its output — somebody
already does this. Displacement, not a gap. The two main articles carry the same
UKEF announcement.

**Suggested reason for contact.** Ask whether the current US arrangement was
priced before the fivefold increase, and who handles the Class 9 paperwork.

**Signal's verdict:** verify before contact. Ranked 2nd on value, strongest on
evidence; the only entry cleared for outreach in the run where pages could be
opened.

## Brief E · NMS International Group — Market Harborough, infrastructure

**Why there may be an opportunity.** Says it is under contract to build 22
district hospitals across Sub-Saharan Africa, with work underway on 12 sites and
over a million square feet under construction. Each site needs plant, steel
frame, fit-out and medical equipment landed on it.

**Evidence** (all search-result summaries; no page opened)
- 22 district hospitals, 12 sites underway, >1,000,000 sq ft —
  `nmsinfrastructure.com` — **no date given**
- Own industrial warehouse next to HQ handling export cargoes, NMSI Logistics
  team doing pre-shipment testing and QC before containerisation —
  `nmsinfrastructure.com/blog/2022/6/28/nms-inaugurates-new-warehouse` — 28 June 2022
- 2024 FEBE Growth 100 listing, "record number of hospital completions" —
  `nmsinfrastructure.com/blog/2024/6/15/...` — 15 June 2024, dates the listing

**Likely angle.** Not warehousing — they run their own and do their own QC and
containerisation. The part still bought is the international leg: ocean and air
freight, oversized and mixed loads, clearing at African ports.

**Uncertainty.** **They already do much of this in-house.** **Nothing dates the
programme** — the only dated corroboration is two years old, so how much of the
22 hospitals remains is unknown. NMS arranges the financing as well as the
build, so freight may already be committed inside it.

**Suggested reason for contact.** Ask who moves the containers once they leave
Market Harborough, whether that is tendered per project, and whether freight
sits inside the project financing.

**Signal's verdict:** monitor, do not call — ranked **last of 5**. A human
reviewer of the same evidence called it the **strongest** opportunity in the
set. The two disagree and the salesperson's view settles it.

## Grading questionnaire

The ten questions asked for, plus three marked **[added]**.

1. Which of these five would you actually call? (A / B / C / D / E / none)
2. Why — and for the ones you would not call, why not?
3. How does this compare with what you could have found yourself in half an hour?
4. What is missing that you would need before picking up the phone?
5. Would this save you meaningful research time? (No / a little / an hour or two
   a week / a lot) — and what would you no longer have to do?
6. Would you trust the evidence enough to contact the company on it? (No / only
   after checking myself / yes)
7. Which entry is strongest, and why?
8. Which is weakest, and why?
9. Would you use something that produced this regularly? (No / maybe / yes) —
   how often would be useful?
10. Would it be worth paying for — and roughly what? A number you would not
    blink at, and a number you would refuse.
11. **[added]** Did the links check out? If a page does not say what the brief
    says it says, that is the most useful answer on the page.
12. **[added]** Where do you disagree with the tool's own verdict? It cleared
    none of these for a call, and ranked NMS last where a human ranked it first.
13. **[added]** Is it worth anything to be told we looked at Winbro and Slack &
    Parr and found nothing for you?

Plus: anything else.
