## 14. Contradictions discovered

Five caveats carried on reported rows, each visible in the output rather than
smoothed away:

- **Maeving** exports since 2023 and ships ~half its output — displacement, not
  greenfield. (This corrected a wrong inference in the original fixture.)
- **Baltex** — five outlets, one HSBC release: one independent source. At 60%
  export with agents in four territories, only the US aerospace lane is new.
- **Bramble** — the new hub closes the storage pitch the signal appears to open.
- **deVOL** — a long-established exporter has incumbent forwarders; the award is
  not the trigger, the new markets are.
- **deVOL** — the Shepshed factory purchase is undated, so carried as context.

## 15. False positives prevented

1. **Brambles Ltd → Bramble Group** — rejected at the gate on country and
   industry conflict.
2. **A model asserting attribution** — structurally impossible: the claim schema
   has no verdict field, and a claim supplying no identity attributes fails
   validation before identity is even considered.
3. **Contraction sold as growth** — polarity is validated against the claim
   text. Redundancy language declared `demand_increasing` is flagged unless the
   rationale addresses it.

## 16. Opportunities missed

- **NMS International**: a FY2023 filing showing £159m turnover, up 93% — real
  and material, but 20 months old, so outside the window. If it is still
  growing at that rate the trigger exists and we have not found the current
  evidence for it.
- **Maeving's £8m raise** (September 2025) sits just outside the 12-month
  window and is carried by neither run.
- **Hiring signals are entirely unexploited.** The registry now has five
  recruitment sources scored as authoritative for hiring, and no query asks
  about vacancies. For a freight forwarder, warehouse and transport hiring is a
  direct demand signal.

## 17. Research cost

**Zero provider credits. £0.** No Apollo call, no paid search, no LLM call —
extraction ran from the captured corpus. Pipeline time 5 ms; the cost that
matters is the 14 search round-trips.

Cost controls held: search reached only 7 of 9 companies, and the two
disqualified ones cost nothing.

## 18. Remaining failure modes

1. **Verification is still the binding cap.** Everything stops at 70 and Medium
   confidence until page fetching is available. This is now the single largest
   constraint on output quality.
2. **The LLM extractor has never run against a real model.** It is fully
   implemented and tested against stubbed responses, but no API key is
   available in this environment, so its real-world behaviour — particularly
   how faithfully it reports identity attributes — is untested.
3. **Extraction quality still bounds identity quality.** An extractor that
   omits `statedGeography` degrades every verdict to "unresolved". Safe, but it
   loses real signals silently.
4. **Registry coverage remains partial at 89 entries.** Better than v2, but the
   long tail of regional and trade publishers is unbounded, and each unknown
   host still costs a tier.
5. **Topic assignment is the extractor's judgement.** Mislabelling a claim's
   topic silently changes the tier it is scored at. Nothing validates it.
6. **Polarity validation is lexical.** It catches obvious contradictions
   ("redundancies" declared as growth) and will miss anything phrased
   euphemistically — "footprint optimisation", "strategic review".
7. **Geography conflict is still country/town only.** A genuinely multi-site
   multinational could trip a false collision.
