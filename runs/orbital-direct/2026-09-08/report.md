## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Why 7 and not 20.** Two reasons, in order of size. First, the
environment blocked source verification, so candidates that would
normally have been confirmed or killed by opening two or three pages
could not be resolved and were dropped rather than guessed at. Second —
and this would apply in a working environment too — the discoverable
signal set for this client's geography is dominated by two kinds of story
that are largely useless to a freight forwarder: enterprise logistics
projects (Aldi's £500m Bardon distribution centre, Tesco at Hinckley,
Bleckmann at Lutterworth) which are far outside Orbital Direct's reach and
run their own logistics, and awards coverage, which recognises exporting
that is already happening rather than a change that creates a new need.
Padding to 20 would have meant listing companies whose only qualification
is being a Midlands manufacturer.

**Strongest signal type this run: trade finance.** The two best rows
(Maeving, Baltex) were both found through export finance announcements —
UK Export Finance and HSBC facilities taken out specifically to fund
export growth. This is a sharper signal than it first appears: a company
that has just borrowed against future export volume has committed to
shipping more, on a known timetable, and the announcement usually names
the destination markets. For a freight forwarder this is close to an
ideal trigger, and it is a source category worth searching deliberately
every run — UKEF, HSBC/Lloyds/NatWest trade finance releases, Innovate UK
grants, regional growth funds.

**Weakest signal type: awards.** Four of the seven rows are King's Award
for Enterprise (International Trade) winners. The methodology rates awards
as low-strength, and that rating held up — an award confirms an established
exporter, which means an incumbent forwarder almost certainly exists.
They are included as probable opportunities because ICP fit is excellent
and proximity is unusual (deVOL is in Loughborough itself, Zeeko and CMS
Cepcor in Coalville), not because the award is a reason to call.

**Pattern worth noting.** The best-fitting prospects for this client are
not the region's largest logistics stories but its mid-sized exporting
manufacturers — £5m–£50m turnover, shipping physical goods to the EU and
US, too small for an in-house customs team. Every enterprise-scale
logistics story surfaced by search was a poor fit or an outright
competitor. A future run should exclude enterprise DC news at the query
stage rather than filtering it out later.

**Limitations.** Beyond the verification blocker: no decision maker was
confirmed for any row (public reporting names founders and directors, but
the operations, supply chain or logistics owner — the right person for
this client — was not publicly identifiable for any candidate); no
company was confirmed at Companies House; and the ICP itself rests on
third-party descriptions of the client rather than the client's own site.
